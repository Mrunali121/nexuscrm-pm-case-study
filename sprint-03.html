Sprint 3: a Dependency Risk That Materialised

A shared component slips by two days. Here is what the PM did.

Duration: Weeks 6–7 Sprint Goal: Sales reps can manage deals through a kanban pipeline with real-time stage changes.

Committed Stories

ID Story Points Owner
NX-9 Kanban pipeline view 8 Frontend Dev
NX-10 Drag-and-drop stage changes, no reload 8 Frontend Dev + Backend Dev A
NX-11 Deal value roll-ups per stage/rep 5 Backend Dev B

Committed points: 21

Dependency Risk That Materialized

This sprint depended on a WebSocket-based real-time update service that Backend Dev A was also responsible for as a shared platform component — a dependency flagged in the Dependency Register (D-02) at sprint planning.

What happened: the WebSocket service took 3 days longer than estimated, because it needed to also support the audit-logging requirement (NX-14) coming in Sprint 4 — an interaction the team hadn't fully scoped at planning time.

PM response: 1. Convened Backend Dev A, Frontend Dev, and Engineering Director on Day 5 to assess real impact rather than letting it run silently. 2. Identified that NX-11 (deal value roll-ups) did not depend on the WebSocket service and could proceed in parallel — reordered Backend Dev B's work to keep the team fully utilized while the blocker was resolved. 3. Renegotiated: NX-10 completed 2 days into Sprint 4 instead of end of Sprint 3, with the Sponsor informed in the weekly status report rather than surprised at the sprint review.

Outcome

Sprint goal substantially met — pipeline view and value roll-ups shipped on time; drag-and-drop stage changes landed 2 days into Sprint 4 with no impact on the Release 1 date, because Sprint 4 had planned slack that absorbed it.

Lesson carried forward

Shared platform components (like the WebSocket service) are now flagged explicitly at sprint planning with their full list of downstream consumers, not just the current sprint's — this gap is captured as an action item in the Retrospective.
