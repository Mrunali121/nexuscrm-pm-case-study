Retrospective

What worked, what did not, and who owns each action.

Format: Start / Stop / Continue, run at project close (Week 18) with the full 9-person team plus the Data Privacy Officer.

What went well

  • Locking Must/Should/Could priorities at the charter stage (R-06 mitigation) meant scope trade-offs during the project — like the PDF export deferral in Sprint 6 — were fast, low-drama decisions instead of late renegotiations.
  • Embedding Sales power-users from requirements workshops through UAT caught real workflow issues early (FR-09, drag-and-drop performance) and drove strong Day-1 adoption — 14/14 users active with the spreadsheet workflow fully retired within 2 weeks of Release 1.
  • Booking external dependencies (audit firm, legal review) with deliberate buffer time meant neither one caused a schedule slip, even though both surfaced real issues (I-01, I-02, and the audit's minor findings).
  • Weekly status reports with explicit risk/issue callouts kept the Sponsor and Steering Committee informed without needing extra ad-hoc meetings.

What didn't go well

  • Shared platform components (like the WebSocket service behind Sprint 3) weren't mapped against all their downstream consumers at the time they were built — only found the audit-logging interaction reactively, causing Issue I-01.
  • Third-party library licensing wasn't a standing check at sprint planning, which meant the PDF export licensing issue (I-02) was discovered mid-sprint rather than anticipated.
  • Release 2's UAT group (Customer Success) was brought in later than Sales — in hindsight, earlier CS involvement during Sprint 5–6 requirements refinement could have caught reporting-view feedback sooner.

Action items

Action Owner Status
Map all downstream consumers for any shared platform component before it's built, not just the current sprint's PM + Eng Director Adopted as a standing design-phase step
Add third-party licensing check as a standing sprint-planning item whenever a new dependency is introduced PM Adopted
Bring secondary user groups (like CS) into requirements refinement at the same stage as primary users, not just at UAT PM Adopted for next project
Keep the "buffer time on external dependencies" practice — it protected the schedule twice PM Carried forward as standard practice

Outcome

Both releases shipped on schedule (Week 8, Week 16). 0 critical bugs at either go-live. Duplicate contact rate came in at 1.8% against a 3% target. Legacy system decommissioned cleanly in Week 18 with no rollback needed.
