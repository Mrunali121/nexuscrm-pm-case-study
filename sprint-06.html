Sprint 6: a Scope Trade-off Under Deadline Pressure

An unplanned legal review threatens a Should-priority feature.

Duration: Weeks 11–12 Sprint Goal: Leadership can export reports and view rep/team-level performance.

Committed Stories

ID Story Points Owner
NX-16 Report export (CSV, PDF) 5 Backend Dev A
NX-17 Rep-level and team-level performance views 5 Frontend Dev + Backend Dev B

Committed points: 10 (lighter sprint by design — team capacity partly reserved for the upcoming external security audit prep ahead of Sprint 7)

Scope Trade-off Under Deadline Pressure

Mid-sprint, PDF export turned out to require a third-party rendering library that introduced a licensing review — Meridian's legal team needed 5 business days to clear it, which didn't fit inside the sprint.

PM decision process: 1. Assessed options: (a) delay the whole sprint goal for legal clearance, (b) ship CSV export only and defer PDF to Sprint 8 buffer, (c) build a lower-quality in-house PDF export to avoid the licensing question entirely. 2. Consulted Engineering Director: option (c) was rejected — technical debt for a Should-priority feature wasn't worth it. 3. Brought (a) vs (b) to the Sponsor with the trade-off stated plainly: CSV covers the actual reporting need leadership raised in requirements workshops; PDF was a nice-to-have add-on. 4. Sponsor chose (b). PDF export moved to the Sprint 8 buffer, contingent on legal clearance landing in time — it did, and PDF export shipped in Sprint 8 as planned.

Outcome

CSV export and both performance views shipped on schedule. PDF export shipped one sprint later with zero impact on the Release 2 date, because it was never on the critical path once deferred.

Lesson carried forward

Third-party library licensing checks are now a standing item at sprint planning whenever a new dependency is introduced, not something discovered mid-sprint — captured as an action item in the Retrospective.
